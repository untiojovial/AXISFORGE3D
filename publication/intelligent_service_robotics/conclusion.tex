We measured, in simulation, what a learned examination order does to a complete parallel-jaw grasp planner, with two confirmations on EGAD objects whose methods, hypotheses and analysis were frozen beforehand. The efficiency result replicated: the learned orders examined \confPoneRatio{} and \nxConeEff{} times as many candidates as the original planner, the robustness and centring terms kept efficiency within the margin of the plain learned order, and the fallback never exceeded its bound. Robustness was non-inferior, fragilely so in the first confirmation. The development gains in robustness and load tolerance did not replicate, including the multi-friction advantage that selected the second method, and with the models inside the planner the learned order no longer doubles planning time but does not reduce it. Frozen foundation-model features did not help, and GPD's classifier was a poor order for this planner. Learned ordering reliably saves planner evaluations; claims of better robustness or faster planning need evidence this study did not find. Real-robot experiments and calibrated contact parameters are the next steps.
