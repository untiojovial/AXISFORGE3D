## P0 mathematical proposal

Use a documented canonical minimization convention: for user objective `f`, set internal `g = s f`, where `s=+1` for minimize and `s=-1` for maximize. Multiply observed outputs and predictive means by s, but retain the same nonnegative standard deviations and variances when changing sign. For a general affine output transform y' = a y + b with nonzero a, transform means as aμ+b, standard deviations as |a|σ, and variances and covariances as a² times their original values. Apply the inverse transform to public predictions and observations; distinguish measurement noise variance from its standard deviation. Inputs have finite bounds `l_i < u_i`, with `z_i=(x_i-l_i)/(u_i-l_i)`. Reject NaN/Inf, dimension mismatches and out-of-domain points. Integer/categorical encodings and mixed optimization require explicit types and are not implied by continuous normalization.

For observations `(X,y)`, constant or explicit mean `m`, kernel `k`, Gaussian observation variances `v_i >= 0`, and numerical jitter `j >= 0`, factor `K_y=K(X,X)+diag(v)+j I = LLᵀ`. Solve `α=L⁻ᵀL⁻¹(y-m(X))`, never form the inverse. For `k_* = k(X,x*)`, latent mean `μ*=m(x*)+k_*ᵀα`, latent variance `σ²*=k(x*,x*)-||L⁻¹k_*||²`. Observation predictive variance adds the specified test observation noise, not jitter. Tiny negative latent variance from roundoff may be clamped only within a documented scale-aware tolerance; material negatives are errors. For an empty dataset return the prior. Duplicate inputs are permitted if factorization succeeds with modeled noise and/or declared numerical jitter; duplicate data do not themselves justify changing statistical noise. If Cholesky fails, follow an explicitly bounded jitter policy, report actual jitter and fail rather than silently return invalid results. Do not optimize hyperparameters implicitly unless its optimizer and bounds are documented.

Log marginal likelihood: `-0.5 rᵀα - sum(log L_ii) - n/2 log(2π)` with `r=y-m(X)`. Exact training is O(n³) time/O(n²) memory; marginal prediction O(n²) per point after factorization, batch covariance differs. Underflow, overflow, hyperparameter extremes and conditioning require explicit validation.

For ARD RBF, `k(x,x')=a² exp(-0.5 Σ_i ((x_i-x'_i)/ℓ_i)²)`, amplitude `a>0`, length scales `ℓ_i>0`. Matérn-5/2 uses `r²=Σ_i ((x_i-x'_i)/ℓ_i)²`, `k=a²(1+√5 r+5r²/3) exp(-√5 r)`. State exact parameterization and test kernel positive semidefiniteness empirically as a regression check, not a proof of PSD.

For noiseless latent Gaussian minimization with incumbent `b`, nonnegative exploration offset `ξ`, `d=b-μ-ξ`, `z=d/σ`, EI to maximize is `d Φ(z)+σ φ(z)` and PI is `Φ(z)` for σ>0. At σ=0, EI=`max(d,0)` and PI uses a documented strict-improvement convention (1 if d>0, else 0). LCB=`μ-β^0.5 σ` is minimized for `β>=0`; equivalently maximize its negative. This EI assumes a fixed incumbent; the observed minimum under noise is biased and is not noisy EI. Stable log-EI and noisy EI require separately sourced formulas and tests. For mixed or constrained problems use feasible incumbents and specified semantics, not a naïve best infeasible observation.

`ask` creates a unique pending token, records exactly what is reserved and consumes budget according to a declared policy; `tell` checks token, inputs, dimensions, direction and status, records failures separately from numeric observations, and rejects duplicates/replays. A serialized checkpoint must preserve observations, model parameters, configuration, pending identities and random generator state for reproducible resume. Parallel asynchronous suggestions require a documented pending-point strategy; repeated greedy EI is not a validated batch method. Predictive and decision RNG state should be isolated or explicitly documented.

## Later designs and limitations

Context `c` must be known at decision time; model `g(x,c)` with explicit decision/context schema, immutable per-evaluation context and no use of future labels. Kernel products/sums are mathematical options, not proof of useful transfer; compare held-out tasks and negative transfer. For constraints, `EI × P(feasible)` equals the joint expectation only when improvement and feasibility are independent under the modeled posterior; correlated constraints, dependencies and no feasible incumbent need explicit treatment. Multitask, multi-fidelity, noisy, high-dimensional, multiobjective, robust, multimodal and parallel methods are separate algorithms, not claims conferred by a generic interface. Multiobjective hypervolume must specify objective signs, dominated reference point and behavior for duplicates. Fidelity choice and evaluation costs are part of the problem definition. Safe BO needs precise safety assumptions and cannot be claimed merely from a feasibility classifier.

## Update: Gardner et al. original paper inspected

Read Gardner, Kusner, Xu, Weinberger and Cunningham, *Bayesian Optimization with Inequality Constraints*, ICML 2014, full proceedings PDF retrieved from the official PMLR archive at [commit 4f959ca831750bc12004b1028b5e1eb52e3f0219](https://github.com/mlresearch/v32/blob/4f959ca831750bc12004b1028b5e1eb52e3f0219/gardner14.pdf), sections 2–6. This does not establish that the unavailable thesis cites or implements this method.

The paper models the scalar objective and constraint separately by conditionally independent GPs and chooses `argmax EI(x) P(c(x)≤λ)`, where the improvement reference is the lowest **observed feasible** objective. Infeasible samples still train both models. For multiple constraints the paper describes a joint feasibility probability; it factors into a product of individual probabilities only under conditional independence of constraints. Objective–constraint independence is a separate assumption. If the models are correlated, in general the correct constrained expected improvement is the joint expectation `E[(b−f(x))_+ 1{all constraints feasible}]`, not the product of marginals. Gaussian posterior constraints permit joint multivariate Gaussian feasibility, but its feasibility probability alone does not resolve objective–constraint dependence.

The printed EI formula in section 2.2 defines `Z=(μ−best)/σ` while defining improvement as `(best−f)_+` and writing `σ[ZΦ(Z)+φ(Z)]`: those signs are inconsistent. The consistent minimization expression uses `Z=(best−μ)/σ` as in the design above. The paper's derivation defines an incumbent as a feasible observation but does not in the main text specify a policy when no feasible observation exists; a production implementation must specify, test and identify that policy rather than attribute it to this paper. The method can query infeasible points and is not a safety guarantee. Its individual GP constraints assume real-valued expensive constraint observations; binary-only feasibility or asynchronous separate evaluations need explicit alternative models.

The reported experiments use two 2D synthetic tasks, each with 30 evaluations comparing uniform search, unconstrained BO and constrained BO; plus LSH and SVM compression tasks compared with uniform search. The paper reports GP hyperparameters chosen by marginal likelihood, and tables summarize standard deviations over 10 runs. These are the authors’ results, not independent runs here; limited baselines, seeds and scenarios do not establish universal advantage. Our final experiments should predefine no-feasible-incumbent behavior, independent versus correlated constraints, noise assumptions and exact budgets, and include infeasibility rate plus conditional objective quality.

## Thesis BO traceability update

The thesis PDF is now available; the extracted text and relevant sections were examined. §2.6 specifies a zero-mean GP with RBF and Matérn-5/2, Gaussian observation noise and posterior mean/latent variance (eqs. 2.1–2.2). Its PI and EI equations assume maximization; they are consistent with `Z=(μ−f_best)/σ`, whereas the proposed core uses minimization after sign conversion. Thesis UCB uses `βσ`, whereas this note defines `sqrt(β)σ`; parameters must be mapped, not treated as numerically equal.

§5.1.2 reports EI and LCB each with 30 initialization samples and 70 optimization steps, versus cAopt with 2 initial and 30 optimization steps; unequal budgets preclude a direct equal-budget efficacy inference. The thesis calls cAopt “Certainty-Aware Optimization” while the inspected BayesOpt revision uses negative standard deviation; the thesis does not establish the implementation version or precise configuration. §5.1.2 describes a volume-based quality proxy using success rate, while §2.3 presents epsilon grasp quality; these are not automatically identical. §5.2.4 says all grasp-success values per object are averaged to make context, without demonstrating they were available before each decision; future-information leakage is possible, not established. §6 says same initial seed for compared approaches, but a fixed seed alone does not quantify variability. Cited BO sources include thesis refs. [17]–[24], BayesOpt [32], and multi-solution grasp exploration [1]; primary-source review beyond the items explicitly recorded here remains pending. ## Implemented core: scoped validation and limitations

The preliminary independent GP uses fixed kernel parameters and Cholesky solves. GP mean and joint covariance agree with scikit-learn on one fixed two/three-point case to `1e-10`; LogEI agrees with 100-digit mpmath at standardized improvements -20, -10, -3, 0 and 2 within `1e-8`. These are targeted checks, not a full scientific validation. Predictive marginal variances use built-in kernel diagonals, avoiding quadratic query-query covariance construction; arbitrary custom kernels without a diagonal method fall back to quadratic memory. Exact fit remains cubic in observations. No automatic hyperparameter fitting, calibrated-noise model or comprehensive precision study is claimed.

A single-region adaptive local box maintains length, success/failure counters and explicit restart request, with anisotropic length-scale normalization; this is the search-state component of the local optimizer described below, not a full TuRBO implementation. The standalone Thompson sampler draws jointly correlated latent values over a finite candidate pool; the local optimizer uses this sampler. Mixed-space validation/encoding and sampling exist but require end-to-end integration testing; one-hot encoding represents no learned category similarity. Sobol design enforces power-of-two size. Independent audits and coverage thresholds remain pending.

A sequential local BO loop now uses the single-region state, a fixed Matérn GP, correlated finite-pool Thompson sampling, sparse coordinate perturbations in normalized space, and random restart with global-best retention. It is not a faithful TuRBO implementation. Joint covariance and eigendecomposition cost quadratic memory and cubic time in candidate-pool size. A development benchmark was actually executed on 10D Sphere with five seeds and 25 objective evaluations per method against random search; raw outputs are in `results/local_sphere_development.json` and the runner is `tools/benchmarks/local_sphere.py`. This narrow five-seed test is neither a final benchmark nor proof of general superiority.

Targeted pytest coverage of six BO modules (models, space, acquisitions, sampling, trust_region and local_optimizer) measured 502/528 lines (95.08%) and 185/212 branches (87.26%); 22 targeted tests passed. These are subset numbers and do not imply that the complete scientific core or final quality gate passes. Independent audit and final benchmarks remain required.

### Replicate-based heteroscedastic surrogate

`ReplicatedNoiseGP` is a two-stage plug-in model requiring at least two independent, conditionally Gaussian observations at every exactly identical input. For each group with `n` replicates, unbiased variance `s²`, and `ν=n−1`, it fits a GP to `log(max(s²,floor)) − [ψ(ν/2)+log 2−log ν]`, with observation variance `ψ₁(ν/2)`. Floor clipping is a numerical regularization and invalidates the exact log-chi-square correction for clipped groups; record its scale and frequency. Its separate mean GP fits sample means with empirical observation variances `max(s²,floor)/n`. The returned individual observation variance equals latent mean variance plus `E[exp(log noise)]` under the approximate log-noise posterior. This does not jointly propagate uncertainty about noise into the mean GP, guarantee calibration, identify noise without replicates, or model correlated/non-Gaussian replicate errors. Tests cover known group statistics, variance/count, floors, invalid inputs and a fixed synthetic low/high noise case; the observed small-sample ranking may reverse due to large log-variance uncertainty and is not guaranteed.
